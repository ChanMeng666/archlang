# Public surfaces: the 3D preview, shown beside the plan

The owner's ruling (2026-11-06): competitors ship strong 3D, and ArchLang's existing 3D (the
`archlang-3d` package) must appear on every ArchLang public surface. This page is the plan for
that. It is part of the public-surfaces programme (`README.md`, `cards.md`, `handoff.md`), and every
PS-D decision still holds unless this page changes it.

## 2. How 3D is shown: paired, never instead (PS-D12)

The research is consistent:
- posts that paired a plan with a 3D extrusion or a drafting animation drew the views;
- every pack advises against competing on 3D walkthroughs or photoreal images, which FloorGen,
  OpenPlan3D, Homistry and Maket own;
- the closest peer by thesis (Skopia) has no 3D at all.

So 3D is always shown **beside its plan, from the same source**, as proof that the plan is a real
model of a building. It is never shown as a picture in its own right.

The line every surface uses:

> **Same source, plan and 3D preview.** The 3D preview is extruded from the same `.arch` file by the
> separate archlang-3d package, and checked against the plan: every 3D element comes from the
> plan's elements, and every solid is closed. You can also download it as a validated GLB.

The limits line, always near a 3D view:

> 3D preview: walls, openings, floors, stairs and furniture in flat colours; no pitched roofs yet,
> and no sections, elevations or BIM.

These follow the facts in §3; update them when the package changes. On the playground only the
line's first sentence stays on screen; the rest and the limits line sit behind an ⓘ (PG-D4, owner
2026-11-07, `docs/plans/playground-minimal/`).

## 3. What the package does today (facts, 2026-11-06)

**What it builds:** `@chanmeng666/archlang-3d` 0.4.0 (X3; 0.3.0 before it) builds the following
from a compiled plan:
- the slab and per-room floor finishes;
- walls taken from the core's own joinery, with sills and lintels;
- doors, windows and glass;
- curved walls;
- demolished walls as see-through ghosts;
- every catalogued furniture family, in material colours from the palette the 2D drawing uses;
- straight and turning stairs as stepped solids with landings, their treads on the plan's tread
  lines (the core's `treadCount`), climbing to the floor above (X3);
- storeys.

**Stairs, as built (X3; ruling delegated 2026-11-06):**
- **One riser per drawn tread, plus one at each flight's head.** The 3D preview keeps the plan's
  treads so the two agree (PS-D12), and the riser height follows from the storey: it is a ledgered
  default (`stair-risers-from-treads`), not a design figure. The core draws a flight's treads at a
  nominal 280 mm going, whatever the storey height, so several examples come out with 245–273 mm
  risers: two-storey 245 (2700 / 11), townhouse and garden-house 250 (3000 / 12), hillside-villa
  273 (3000 / 11); turning-stair 229 (2750 / 12). The core's lint assumes a riser of at most 220 mm
  (`stairMaxRiseMm`, `W_STAIR_TOO_STEEP`) and checks a flight's run against the shortest run that
  climbs the storey; it never compares the treads the plan draws with the storey height. That lint
  is backlog ST.1.
- **No `void` over a stair, no hole above it.** Where the storey above writes no `void` over the
  stair (townhouse, garden-house, turning-stair; hillside-villa's `v_gallery` stands beside its
  stair, south of it, not over it), its slab is solid, so the stair's head runs under it.
  two-storey's `gallery` void is the one that opens over its stair. That is the plan as written; the 3D preview cuts only the voids the plan states.

**Outputs:**
- a GLB, checked by the Khronos validator with 0 errors and 0 warnings, its hash pinned;
- a self-contained replay HTML: the plan draws itself, then rises into 3D (17 key frames pinned);
- an MP4 of that replay, through Playwright, SwiftShader and ffmpeg (its bytes do not repeat
  between machines);
- free orbit, with presets Iso, Top, N, E, S and W.

**Gaps a viewer will notice:**
- roofs are a hidden flat slab;
- no still-image export (posters come from `gen:3d-assets`).

### Stage B: assets (real output, reproducible)

- **X4 · `gen:3d-assets`.** A script in `packages/archlang-3d` that, for a fixed list of showcase
  plans, writes:
  - a **poster** (1600×900 PNG, from `__seek(1)` at the iso end frame; light theme);
  - a **replay MP4** (1280×720, about 10 s, `--video`);
  - a **GLB**.
  It runs on the pinned Chromium with SwiftShader, as `check-3d` does.
  - The plans: one-room or studio, furnished-flat, laneway-house, knob-flat, villa-rotonda
    (showcase), and one storeyed plan after X2.
  - Posters and MP4s are generated, not hand-made. They are committed under
    `docs-site/public/3d/` within a size budget: MP4 ≤ 1.5 MB each, poster ≤ 200 KB as WebP plus a
    PNG fallback.
  - A pixel-tolerance check, not a hash, guards the posters (MP4 bytes do not repeat). A
    `gen:3d-assets` drift check compares frame hashes from the existing key-frame machinery.
  - **As built (PR #519):**
    - The list in this repository is studio, furnished-flat, laneway-house, knob-flat, two-storey
      and turning-stair, each with a poster, MP4 and GLB.
    - villa-rotonda lives in the showcase repository, so it is not on that list. X8 generates it
      there with the external form, `npm run gen:3d-assets -- --out <dir> --plan
      villa-rotonda=<path.arch>`, from a checkout of this one.
    - The two storeyed plans' posters show storey 1 alone so their stairs are in sight, and each
      adds the assembled frame as `poster-full`.
    - Their walls stop under the slab of the storey above (the storey joint), so the assembled view
      has no sliver at the joint.
    - Assets change only when what they show changes (`packages/archlang-3d/DEVELOPMENT.md`).
    - The poster check is exact in the environment that rendered the poster. Elsewhere it is
      advisory, within the pixel tolerance.
- **X4b · More assets.** The docs home gallery's other plans, and a 2D plan PNG for every plan.
  - **As built:**
    - bungalow, courtyard-house, hillside-villa, corner-cafe and library join the list with
      `video: false`: a poster and GLB each, no MP4. hillside-villa's poster shows storey 1, with
      `poster-full`, as the stairs do. Every file is within its budget.
    - Every plan gets `<name>.plan.png`, the core's drawing of the poster's storey through the core's
      PNG path, the bytes `arch preview` writes (1600 px wide). The gallery's plans declare `paper`,
      so theirs carry the sheet: the core has no plan-only output (`plan.sheet` in the manifest).
    - The manifest records the plan PNG's SHA-256 and its decoded pixels' hash. The check
      re-rasterises it to that hash, exactly on every platform, as `test/visual.test.ts` does. It
      also holds each entry to its row: an MP4 exactly when `video`, and a plan PNG.
