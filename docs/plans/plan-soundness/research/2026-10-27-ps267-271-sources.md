## PS2.67 · A catalogued fixture drawn under its smallest size

**Sources.**
- Which?, "UK mattress sizes: bed and mattress size guide", 8 May 2026,
  https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/mattresses/article/uk-mattress-sizes-bed-mattress-size-guide-aDx4r9b4T99o
  (table "Standard UK bed and mattress sizes") [V, a consumer source; it names no standard]: small
  single 75 × 190 cm, single 90 × 190, small double 120 × 190, double 135 × 190, king 150 × 200, super
  king 180 × 200.
- GB/T 3328-2016 家具 床类主要尺寸 (Furniture: main sizes of beds), issued 2016-12-13, in force
  2017-07-01, replaces GB/T 3328-1997 (https://www.chinesestandard.net/PDF.aspx/GBT3328-2016) [S]: the
  standard exists; its size table was NOT read.
- Shower trays: no sizing standard found. A UK retailer states 700 mm is typically the narrowest tray
  width sold; Delabie's stainless range lists recessed trays at 700 × 700 and 800 × 800 mm; BS EN
  14527:2016 is a product standard, not a sizing rule [S, search summaries of retailer and
  manufacturer pages]. BS 6465 and Neufert were searched for and not reached.
- Examination couches: product listings cluster at 1800–1930 mm long × 600 mm wide (Red Cross items
  catalogue, "Couch, examination, adjustable headrest", 1930 × 600 × 800 mm,
  https://itemscatalogue.redcross.int/health--3/medical-equipment--15/medical-equipment--78/couch-examination-adjustable-headrest--XMEQCOUC.aspx)
  [S]. BS 2838 (fixed-height examination couches) is paywalled and was not read; HBN 00-03 was not
  reached.

**Figures and basis.** Bed length (head to foot) at least 1900 mm, the length of every UK standard size
below king; bed width at least 750 mm (the UK small single). These are nominal mattress sizes, so the
drawn footprint (the frame) of a real bed is at least that. The shower tray has no sourced minimum: a
700 × 700 tray is a real product, so QS5's 700 × 700 shower is under no figure found. An exam couch has
no catalogue family (QK2 draws it as `bed`; the catalogue's `couch` is living-room seating); drawn as
`bed`, QK2's 1500 mm couch is under the bed length.

**Profile differences.** Default = the UK figures above (the only sizes read). `us-residential-advisory`:
not set (a US single is reported as 99 × 190 cm second-hand; not verified). `cn-residential-advisory`:
not set until GB/T 3328-2016's table is read.

**Recommended rule shape.** Measure each `bed` and `double_bed` (and `bunk_bed`, a stacked single) on
its catalogued axes: the length along the head-to-foot axis, the width across it (a transposed footprint
already warns as `W_FOOTPRINT_TRANSPOSED`, so read the longer side as the length). Fire when the length
is under 1900 mm or the width under 750 mm; never for `crib` or `cot`, which are short by design.
Message, in words: "the bed is 1500 mm long, under the 1900 mm of the shortest standard bed (a UK
single, 90 × 190 cm): 400 mm short". Stage `fixtures`, layer `validity`, family `furniture`. Corpus
case: a 1500 × 700 `bed` (the clinic's couch shape); clean twin: the same bed at 900 × 1900. Examples
likely to move: none (every example bed is at least 1900 long and 1000 wide: `terrace-row` 1400 × 1900,
`two-storey` 1000 × 2000, `tiny-house` 2000 × 1400 rotated). The row-6 clinic warns; the row-6 studio
does not (no shower minimum).

**Evasion check.** Cheapest evasion: retype the piece as a family with no minimum (`couch`, `bench`,
`table`), which draws the wrong symbol; the 700 shower stays legal as it is. A rule cannot resist a
retype; the honest fix is a catalogued exam-couch family with its own sourced minimum (a separate card;
BS 2838 or HBN 00-03 would source it).

**Status.** Ready in part: the bed minimum (1900 × 750) is sourced. Blocked for the shower tray: no
sourced minimum found (BS 6465, Neufert, retailer and manufacturer ranges searched; 700 × 700 trays are
sold). The exam-couch figure waits for a catalogue family and a source. The card's acceptance "the
row-6 studio warns" cannot be met on a sourced basis and needs restating.

## PS2.68 · Chairs back to back with no room to push back

**Sources.** Searched: Time-Saver Standards for Interior Design, Panero & Zelnik *Human Dimension &
Interior Space*, Neufert *Architects' Data*. None of the three was reached online. What was found is
trade guidance, and it disagrees [S, search summaries]:
- Mity-Lite, "Restaurant table spacing" (https://mitylite.com/blog/restaurant-table-spacing): 18–24 in
  (457–610 mm) behind occupied chairs.
- The Restaurant Warehouse
  (https://therestaurantwarehouse.com/blogs/restaurant-equipment/cross-back-chairs) and
  climbtheladder.com: 18 in (457 mm) between the backs of chairs at separate tables, as a casual-dining
  minimum.
- Oak Street Mfg.
  (https://www.oakstreetmfg.com/the-ultimate-restaurant-seating-layout-guide-with-real-numbers/): 12 in
  (305 mm) between chairs back to back.
- TigerChef
  (https://www.tigerchef.com/answers/6492703/How-much-spacing-should-I-allow-between-restaurant-tables):
  42 in between square tables for chairs back to back, about 6 in (152 mm) to push out.
- Dimensions.com, "Dining Room Clearances" (https://www.dimensions.com/element/dining-room-clearances):
  a sitting zone of 18–24 in behind the table edge; it names no source.

**Figures and basis.** None adopted: 152, 305 and 457 mm are each given as the minimum by a different
vendor, and none is a primary or recognised secondary reference. QC2's 100 mm is under all three.

**Profile differences.** None to state.

**Recommended rule shape (when sourced).** For two seats at different tables (each "at" its table as
`W_SEAT_FACING_AWAY` reads it) whose backs face each other, measure the clear gap between the two back
edges; fire under the allowance. Message, in words: "two chairs at neighbouring tables stand back to
back with 100 mm between them, under the N mm needed to push a chair back". Stage `fixtures`, layer
`validity`. Corpus case: QC2's two rows at 100 mm; clean twin: the rows at the allowance. Café examples
to re-measure when the figure lands.

**Evasion check.** Cheapest evasion: rotate one chair a few degrees, or move it out of its table's
reach so it no longer counts as "at" a table; the rule should read the seat's back edge whatever its
table.

**Status.** Blocked: no sourced figure found (Time-Saver Standards, Panero & Zelnik, Neufert searched
for and not reached; trade guides give 6, 12 and 18 in and disagree). Unblock with a read of Panero &
Zelnik (dining and restaurant section) or Neufert (restaurants).

## PS2.69 · Furniture in front of an exterior exit

**Sources.**
- International Residential Code, R311.3 "Floors and landings at exterior doors" (2012 and 2021 text,
  quoted in Indiana DHS, "Section R311.3",
  https://secure.in.gov/dhs/files/Section-R311.3-Floors-and-Landings-at-Exterior-Doors.pdf) [S]: "a
  landing or floor on each side of each exterior door", of a width "not less than the door served",
  and "a dimension of not less than 36 inches (914 mm) measured in the direction of travel".
  *Added 2026-10-28 (D27 ratification):* the same R311.3 text, read verbatim in a second,
  independent state copy: Montana DLI, 2021 IRC means-of-egress training,
  https://bsd.dli.mt.gov/_docs/Training/2021-IRC-Means-of-Egress-Thursday-AM.pdf [S]. Both copies
  carry the exception for an exterior balcony under 60 sq ft reached only through a door; the rule
  already skips every door onto an `outdoor balcony` (any size). Still [S], not the ICC original,
  but two independent copies agree.
- International Building Code (2021), 1010.1.6 "Landings at doors" (ICC committee text,
  https://www3.iccsafe.org/cs/committeeArea/pdf_file/BU_18_38_21.pdf) [S]: landing width not less than
  the stair or the door, whichever is greater; not less than 44 in (1118 mm) in the direction of
  travel; 36 in for Group R-3, U and within R-2 units.
- 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design, 404.2.4.1 and Table 404.2.4.1
  (https://www.corada.com/documents/2010ADAStandards/404) [V, a transcription of the Standards]: front
  approach, pull side, 60 in (1525 mm) perpendicular to the doorway plus 18 in (455 mm) beyond the
  latch side; push side, 48 in (1220 mm), plus 12 in (305 mm) beside the latch if a closer and a latch
  are provided; 404.2.3, a clear width of 32 in (815 mm).
- UK, Approved Document M: the 1500 × 1500 mm level landing is OUTSIDE the entrance, and the 300 mm
  nib is a width beside the leading edge, not a depth (MHCLG, FAQ on ADM Vol 1 and Vol 2,
  https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/648232/FAQ_ADM_Vol1_and_Vol2.pdf)
  [S]. No inside clear depth found. China: not searched (budget).

**Figures and basis.** Clear floor in front of the inside face of an exterior door, measured
perpendicular to the doorway, over a width not less than the door's clear width. Default 914 mm (IRC
R311.3; also IBC's figure for dwellings): the lesser figure, so a normal layout never trips it.

**Profile differences.** Default and `us-residential-advisory`: 914. A non-dwelling plan (a clinic, a
café, an office) could take IBC's 1118 when it is judged as non-residential; the acceptance does not
need it. `accessibility-advisory`: 1220 when the door swings out (the inside is the push side), 1525
when it swings in (the inside is the pull side), from the ADA table. `uk-residential-advisory` and
`cn-residential-advisory`: no figure found; keep the default.

**Recommended rule shape.** For each door with the exterior on one side (the circulation model's
exterior doors), take a strip on the inside face, the door's clear width wide and the egress depth
deep, built from the wall's shape, not a box. Subtract the furniture footprints that overlap it with
positive area, and fire when the widest clear run across the strip is under the required width,
max(the door's clear width, `minDoorWidthMm`). Message, in words: "the sofa stands 500 mm inside the
main exit and leaves 600 mm of its 1200 mm clear; an exit wants 914 mm of clear floor in front of it".
Stage `fixtures`, layer `validity`; a sibling of `W_DOORWAY_BLOCKED`, which keeps its 300 / 450
approach for every door. Corpus case: QK4's sofa 500 mm off the façade over 600 of a 1200 exit; clean
twin: the sofa 1000 mm off. Examples likely to move: any example with a piece within 914 mm of the
inside of a front or garden door (re-measure the corpus; expect few).

**Evasion check.** Cheapest evasions: narrow the exit door to the width left (resisted: the required
width is floored at `minDoorWidthMm`, and the door-width rule still reads the door), or make the exit an
`opening` so it is no longer a door (the rule should read exterior openings too, or PS2.16's escape
checks will). Sliding the piece back to 914 mm is the intended repair, not an evasion.

**Status.** Ready: 914 mm default (IRC R311.3 [S]); 1220 / 1525 under `accessibility-advisory` (ADA
404.2.4.1 [V]). Verify R311.3 in the ICC text before the figure ships in a message (D3).
*Ratified 2026-10-28 (D27, owner's delegation):* the 914 mm ships on the two agreeing state copies
above; the ICC text was still not read.

## PS2.70 · `W_GARAGE_TOO_NARROW` judges depth too

**Sources.**
- Monmouthshire County Council, *Supplementary Planning Guidance: Domestic Garages*, January 2013
  (https://www.monmouthshire.gov.uk/app/uploads/2024/07/Domestic-Garage-SPG-Jan-2013.pdf) [V]: ¶12,
  "garages shall have minimum clear internal dimensions of 3.0m x 6.0m, as recommended in Manual for
  Streets (Appendix A note 3)"; ¶25, the same for new single garages; ¶17 and ¶27, a double parallel
  garage "5.5m x 6.0m (width x length)"; ¶21, a car port "a minimum roof length of 5.0m, and a minimum
  roof width of 2.8m" (no door-opening allowance, ¶20).
- Buckinghamshire Council, *Parking guidance for new developments* (local planning guidance, updated
  22 September 2022) [V]: §9 Table 11 "Minimum garage dimensions", 6.0 m long × 3.0 m wide; §8 Table 9
  "Minimum car parking dimensions", 5.0 × 2.8 m.
- Concurring [S, search summaries]: North Lanarkshire and Ribble Valley highways count a garage as a
  space only at 3.0 × 6.0 m internal.
- Manual for Streets (DfT 2007), Appendix A note 3: cited by Monmouthshire, not read itself. US (IRC)
  and Neufert: not searched (budget).

**Figures and basis.** Clear internal depth along the vehicle door's axis: 6000 mm for a garage (room
to walk round the car and open its door), 5000 mm for the car alone (a bay or car-port length). The
same depth serves a double garage (Monmouthshire ¶17). Width cross-check: the sources' bay width is
2800 and their garage width 3000; the shipped `CAR_BAY_WIDTH_MM` is 2700, deliberately under 3000 so a
5500 double (¶17's own figure, and `hillside-villa`'s) stays silent, and 100 mm under the 2800 bay
width. Noted, not changed by this card.

**Profile differences.** Default 5000 (the car's own length: a garage shallower than a parking bay
cannot hold a car at all; calibrated like the width rule, so a normal layout never trips it).
`uk-residential-advisory`: 6000 deep and 3000 wide per bay (Manual for Streets via Monmouthshire;
Buckinghamshire); that profile is outside the never-worse comparison (D12). `us-` and
`cn-residential-advisory`: no figure found; keep the default.

**Recommended rule shape.** For a rectangular garage (polygons skipped, as today), the depth axis is
perpendicular to the wall holding its `door garage`; with no vehicle door, the room's long side.
Measure the clear depth between wall faces on that axis and fire under the figure, whatever the bay
count. Message, in words: "the garage is 3000 mm deep from its vehicle door, under the 5000 mm a parked
car needs: 2000 mm short". A second code (for example `W_GARAGE_TOO_SHALLOW`) or a second clause of the
same rule, appended after the width check in the per-room order; stage as the width rule. Corpus case:
QH5's garage, 3000 deep along its door's axis; clean twin: 6000 deep. `examples/hillside-villa.arch`
(a 5500 × 6200 room, its vehicle door on the north side) stays silent under the default; re-measure its
clear depth under `uk-residential-advisory` (6200 less the wall half-thicknesses, against 6000).
`examples/garden-house.arch`'s garage to re-measure the same way.

**Evasion check.** Cheapest evasions: move the vehicle door to another wall so a longer side becomes
the depth (the width check then reads the other side, so a 3000 × 3600 garage still fails one of the
two), drop `uses garage` (the room then reads as something else; out of reach of any garage rule), or
delete the vehicle door (the long-side fallback still reads it).

**Status.** Ready: default 5000; `uk-residential-advisory` 6000 deep × 3000 wide (Monmouthshire SPG
2013 ¶12, ¶21, ¶25 [V]; Buckinghamshire Tables 9 and 11 [V]).

## PS2.71 · A bed head across a window: lint or taste

**Decision: taste (a criterion in the taste score), not a lint rule.**

**Reasoning, under D2 and the two precedents.**
- `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW` is validity because a tall unit physically blocks the window's light and
  view. A bed is low; a headboard under a sill blocks neither.
- `W_SEAT_FACING_AWAY` is validity because a chair facing away from its table cannot be used as a
  place at that table. A bed with its head under a window is slept in as intended.
- The reasons against a bed head under a window are draught and cold, reaching across the bed to open
  the window or draw a curtain, light on the sleeper's face, and feng shui. Each is a preference whose
  weight varies with climate, window type and culture, which is D2's own description of taste. No code
  found in this record forbids it, and the egress codes read here do not forbid furniture under an
  escape window.
- Small rooms routinely put the bed head under the only window when no other wall takes it; a lint
  warning there would be a false positive on a buildable plan, where a taste criterion scores it lower
  without failing it.

**Reverse if:** a code or a recognised design reference is found that states it as a requirement, or the
model gains headboard heights and a headboard reaches above the sill (then it is the tall-unit case and
belongs to `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW`).

**What the criterion measures.** For each bed (`bed`, `double_bed`, `bunk_bed`), the head edge is the
catalogued back of the directional piece as its `rotate` places it. The criterion counts the beds whose
head edge lies in a window's band (the window's span along its straight host wall, within
`fixtureWallTolMm` of the wall's centreline, as `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW` builds it) and overlaps that
span with positive length. A bed side-on under a window (its long side in the band, its head on another
wall) is silent; a window whose sill is at or above 2100 mm is not judged. Taste corpus pair: the row-6
apartment's bedroom 2 with the bed head on the window wall (`<case>.arch`) and the same bed turned to
the solid wall (`<case>.good.arch`); both twins lint clean. Like every taste criterion it needs a cited
source for its weight before it ships (D3); this record found none, so the criterion's card must name
one or record the convention as unsourced.

**Status.** Decided: taste.
