# PS2.50 as built (2026-10-26)

Record for card PS2.50: `W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM` on a piece end-on or side-on to a wall, PR #267.
Branch `fix/lint-back-to-room-side-on-ps250`. Tests: `test/lint-back-to-room-side-on.test.ts`.

## What shipped

- **`W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM` declines** (`src/lint/rules/furniture.ts`) on a piece standing
  side-on or end-on to a wall. Three conditions must all hold:
  1. no walled edge of the piece is a back candidate (`backCandidateEdges`);
  2. the drawn front is off the wall too (`backEdgeForRotate(rotate + 180)` is not walled);
  3. the piece is not one whose back must reach any wall it touches (`backsOntoWall`).

  A bed lengthwise along a wall and a wardrobe touching one with its end are layouts, not backs
  turned to the room.
- **It still fires** in these cases:
  - **The drawn front is on a wall, whatever the aspect.** Examples are a nightstand
    `400x450 rotate 180` and a wardrobe `600x1800 rotate 180`, each facing the wall.
  - **A walled edge could be the back, and the front is on it instead.** Examples are a counter
    facing the wall and a bed with its foot on the wall. The `rotate` fix is unchanged.
  - **The piece is `backsOntoWall`.** This covers every plumbed or vented piece (`requiresWall`)
    and the four wall-mounted families (`wallMounted`). They keep the size-swap hint.
- **A new catalogue flag, `wallMounted`** (`src/fixtures-catalog.ts`): "its back mounts on a wall
  whenever it touches one". It is set on exactly the radiator, fireplace, TV and EV charger.
  These families are deliberately not `requiresWall`, since they need no services and may stand
  free. But touching a wall end-on is the wrong way round for them, where it is a layout for a
  bed or a wardrobe. `backsOntoWall(category)` is `requiresWall || wallMounted`.
- **Side-on placement has a lint-clean spelling** in the existing grammar: `at (x,y)` or
  `in <room> anchor <edge> flush`, with a `rotate`. It raises no furniture-family code (the test
  checks every code of the family, read off `LINT_RULES`). `flush` matters: without it the anchor
  puts the piece on the room edge, which is a wall centreline, and that raises
  `W_FURNITURE_WALL_COLLISION`. On `origin/main` a crib written side-on raised
  `W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM`, so it had no clean reading.
- **`W_FOOTPRINT_TRANSPOSED` keeps judging `against`** (`src/lint/rules/footprint.ts`). A side-on
  recipe is added to the hint only for a piece deeper than wide that is not `backsOntoWall`: a bed,
  bunk bed, crib or treadmill. The recipe uses the piece's own numbers: `at (x,y) size <w>x<h>
  rotate <r + 90>`. The resolved plan rectangle is already the side-on footprint, so the recipe
  keeps it and turns the symbol a quarter. This holds on any wall: the red team's east-wall probe
  `d01` gets `at (3200,850) size 700x1300 rotate 180`, which raises no furniture code. Inside a
  turned `place`, the size and turn are pulled back into the component's frame and the position
  is left as `(x,y)`. A wide family (TV, bar counter, `sofa_l`, loveseat) gets no recipe, because
  its end on the wall is not side-on. Neither does a `backsOntoWall` one: both keep "the wall it
  backs onto states its turn".
- **Prose updated.** Both catalogue entries (`src/error-catalog.ts`), the table row in
  `docs/furniture.md`, and `docs-site/examples.md` (hillside-villa now raises seven warnings, not
  eight). The plan-soundness `README.md` and `repro/README.md` give the family-apartment count as
  33. The stale bed comment in `examples/tiny-house.arch` is rewritten (comment only). Generated
  files: `docs/error-codes.md`, `llms-full.txt`, `src/context-size.ts` and
  `docs/cli-reference.md`, where the context bundle goes from 137 KB to 138 KB.

## Decisions

1. **Decline when no walled edge is a back candidate: taken, and narrowed twice.**
   - **The drawn front must be off the wall** (red-team round 1, blocker). Without this
     condition, the first version also silenced a piece facing the wall whenever its drawn aspect
     was the inverse of the catalogue's: a nightstand `400x450 rotate 180`, a wardrobe
     `600x1800 rotate 180`, a filing cabinet, a bookshelf, a dresser, a microwave or a bin. That
     is exactly the defect the rule exists for.
   - **The piece must not be `backsOntoWall`.** A basin touching a wall only with its side has
     its back in the room, and its supply and waste are at the back. A radiator, fireplace, TV or
     EV charger touching a wall with its end is mounted wrong.

   The bed and wardrobe the card names satisfy every condition, so the card's result stands.
2. **`W_FOOTPRINT_TRANSPOSED` on a side-on `against` piece: not silenced (a change from the
   default, with this reason).** `against` derives the turn that puts the symbol's back on the
   wall. It refuses `rotate` (`E_FURN_AGAINST`) and `facing` (`E_FACING_CONFLICT`). So a crib
   written `against wall … size 1300x700` really is drawn head-to-wall, its cot 1300 wide and 700
   long. A render beside `at … size 1300x700 rotate 90` shows the first squashed and the second
   drawn side-on correctly. The warning is therefore a true positive about the drawing.

   There were two ways to make that spelling clean, and neither was taken:
   - **Change what an existing spelling draws.** That would be a resolver guess, against ADR 0005,
     and would move every plan that writes it, the holdout `eval/goldens/against-wall-bath`
     included.
   - **Add a clause.** Side-on already has a spelling, so a clause is not unavoidable.

   No grammar was added. The hint gives the spelling with numbers, and the `against` side-on
   clause is left open (below).
3. **Messages stay id-free.** Neither message changed. The new hint text quotes no id, and the
   `_idAuthored` rule is unchanged; both directions are tested.
4. **Corpus cases went into the rule's own test, not `test/defect-corpus`.** The defect corpus
   holds defect/clean twin pairs, each pinned to an audit origin. Most of these cases are guards
   against false positives, with no defect to pin.

   Cases that stay silent:
   - a bed side-on;
   - the hillside wardrobe, `550x1800` end-on, in its three end-on turns;
   - a wardrobe on the same floor and turn as the wall-mounted pieces;
   - a side-on bed under a `place` turn or mirror;
   - a bed, a crib and a treadmill by `at` or `anchor … flush` + `rotate`, raising no furniture
     code at all;
   - the hinted recipe on a north and an east wall, raising no furniture code.

   Cases that still warn:
   - a wardrobe `600x1800 rotate 180` and a nightstand `400x450 rotate 180` facing the wall;
   - a counter facing the wall, with its fix;
   - a bed with its foot on the wall;
   - a basin side-on, with the size hint;
   - the radiator, fireplace, TV and EV charger end-on;
   - a facing counter under a `place` turn or mirror;
   - a transposed `sofa_l`;
   - a crib by `against`, with the recipe in the hint, under a turned `place` (frame-local size
     and turn), and with an authored id quoted in the hint only;
   - a WC, a TV and a bar counter by `against`, with no recipe.

## Open

- **An `against` side-on clause** (not built). The sketch:
  `furniture crib against wall shell offset 1500 lengthwise head start|end`.
  - The piece's long side would stand on the wall face, and `size` would stay the piece's own
    along × depth.
  - `head start|end` would point its back toward the start or end of the wall run. That is
    traversal-relative, like `hinge near start`, so a mirror carries it without a handed flip.

  This is a language form. It would need:
  - a parser and resolver (`placeAgainst` with the plan size swapped, and the turn taken off the
    wall direction ± 90);
  - `fmt`, Plan JSON and `describe()` `_authored`;
  - a `place` transform;
  - a conflict code for a `backsOntoWall` piece;
  - a spec/SKILL line inside the `spec.llm.md` budget;
  - a byte-identity law.

  It is worth building only if authors keep reaching for `against` for side-on pieces. The hint's
  recipe already writes the `at` spelling for them.
- **A piece transposed against a wall is now judged by no rule** when it is neither `fixedAspect`
  nor `backsOntoWall` and its end touches the wall with its front off it (a wardrobe `600x1800`).
  Before, this rule caught it, by accident and in the same words it used for the false positive.
  PS2.53 (a per-family depth ceiling) is the card that catches it.
- **`handoff.md` §4 item 9** still counts "PS2 41 todo"; PS2.50 makes it 40. It was left for the
  coordinator's next count, to keep this PR's handoff edit minimal.
