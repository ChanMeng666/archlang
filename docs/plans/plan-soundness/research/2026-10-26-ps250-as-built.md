# PS2.50 as built (2026-10-26)

Record for card PS2.50: `W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM` on a piece end-on or side-on to a wall. Branch
`fix/lint-back-to-room-side-on-ps250`. Tests: `test/lint-back-to-room-side-on.test.ts`.

## What shipped

- **`W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM` declines** (`src/lint/rules/furniture.ts`) when no walled edge of
  the piece is a back candidate (`backCandidateEdges`) and the piece needs no services
  (`requiresWall` false). A bed lengthwise along a wall and a wardrobe touching one with its end
  are layouts, not backs turned to the room.
- **It still fires** where a walled edge could be the back and the front is on it instead (a
  counter facing the wall, a bed with its foot on the wall; the `rotate` fix is unchanged), and
  for every plumbed or vented piece (`requiresWall`) whatever its layout, with the size-swap hint
  as before.
- **Side-on placement has a lint-clean spelling** in the existing grammar: `in <room> anchor
  <edge>` or `at (x,y)` with a `rotate`. With the decline above, both rules read it as clean (a
  crib `in r anchor top size 1300x700 rotate 90`; a treadmill `anchor left … rotate 0`). On
  `origin/main` the same crib raised `W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM`, so it had no clean reading.
- **`W_FOOTPRINT_TRANSPOSED` keeps judging `against`** (`src/lint/rules/footprint.ts`). For a
  piece that needs no services, its hint now says that `against` always backs a piece onto the
  wall and gives the side-on spelling. A plumbed piece keeps the swap-only hint.
- Both catalogue entries (`src/error-catalog.ts`) say the same; `docs/furniture.md`'s table row
  and `docs-site/examples.md` (hillside-villa now raises seven warnings, not eight) follow.
  Generated: `docs/error-codes.md`, `llms-full.txt`, `src/context-size.ts` and
  `docs/cli-reference.md` (the context bundle goes from 137 KB to 138 KB).

## Decisions

1. **Decline when no walled edge is a back candidate: taken, narrowed to pieces that need no
   services.** The coordinator's default declined for every piece. Narrowed because
   `requiresWall` means services (the catalogue's own definition, `src/fixtures-catalog.ts`): a
   basin or sink touching a wall only with its side, its back in the room, is a real defect,
   because the supply and waste are at its back. It was caught before and is still caught. The
   two pieces the card names (bed, wardrobe) are `directional`, not `requiresWall`, so both
   readings give the card's result. The `requiresWall` branch also keeps
   `examples/courtyard-house.arch`'s washer comment true.
2. **`W_FOOTPRINT_TRANSPOSED` on a side-on `against` piece: changed from the default, with this
   reason.** `against` derives the turn that puts the symbol's back on the wall; `rotate` is
   refused there (`E_FURN_AGAINST`) and so is `facing` (`E_FACING_CONFLICT`). A crib written
   `against wall … size 1300x700` is therefore drawn head-to-wall, its cot 1300 wide and 700
   long. Rendering it next to `at … size 1300x700 rotate 90` shows the first squashed and the
   second drawn correctly side-on. So the warning on the `against` crib is a true positive about
   the drawing, and silencing it would hide the defect the rule exists for. Making `against` draw
   it side-on would mean one of two things. Either `against` would change what it draws for an
   existing spelling (the resolver guessing the author's intent from the aspect, against ADR 0005,
   and moving the drawing of every plan that writes it, such as the holdout
   `eval/goldens/against-wall-bath`), or it would need a new clause. The default asked for neither
   unless unavoidable. It is avoidable: side-on already has a spelling, `in <room> anchor <edge>`
   or `at` with a `rotate`, which is clean once decision 1 lands. So no grammar was added, and
   the `against` side-on clause is left as an open design (below).
3. **Messages stay id-free.** Neither message changed; the new hint text quotes no id, and the
   existing `_idAuthored` rule for the transposed hint is unchanged (tested both ways).
4. **Corpus cases went into the rule's own test, not `test/defect-corpus`.** The corpus holds
   defect/clean twin pairs, each pinned to an audit origin; most of these cases are
   false-positive guards with no defect to pin. Covered: a bed side-on (clean), a wardrobe
   end-on in every turn (clean), a counter facing the wall (warns, with its fix), a bed with its
   foot on the wall (warns), a basin side-on (warns, size hint), a `place` turn or mirror of the
   bed and counter, a transposed `sofa_l` (warns), a crib and a treadmill side-on by `anchor`/`at`
   with a `rotate` (clean under both rules), and a crib side-on by `against` (warns, with the new
   hint; id quoted only when authored), plus a WC by `against` (swap-only hint). Against
   `origin/main`'s `src/`, the five new-behaviour cases fail and the six guards pass.

A sweep of 233 plans (examples, `eval/goldens`, `test/defect-corpus`, `test/recovery-corpus`,
`test/fixtures`, `test/walls-from-rooms`) compared every storey's SVG, `describe()`, `lint()`

| `examples/tiny-house.arch` | `lint()` 2 → 1: loses `W_FIXTURE_BACK_TO_ROOM` on the bed | false positive withdrawn: the bed lies lengthwise with its side on the north wall (`2000x1400 rotate 90`) |

Both plans' SVG (every storey), `describe()` and `compile().diagnostics` are byte-identical, and
no example source was edited. No eval golden moved, so `eval/judge-fixture.json` is untouched
(D19). Re-pinned rows, each the suite's own digest of the new payload: `test/byte-identity-baseline.ts`
(`BASELINE` and `SEMANTIC_BASELINE`, two rows each), `test/while-byte-identity-baseline.ts` and
`test/import-module-lets.test.ts` (two rows each), and `test/taste-digests.test.ts` (three rows
each). Every one of the six taste rows returns byte for byte to its value before PS2.11c/d
(PR #253), which shows that the bed and wardrobe turns moved nothing else the taste report reads.
The defect corpus is unchanged at 59/73.

## Open

- **An `against` side-on clause** (not built). Sketch: `furniture crib against wall shell offset
  1500 lengthwise head start|end`. The piece's long side would stand on the wall face, `size`
  would stay the piece's own along × depth, and `head start|end` would point its back toward
  the wall run's start or end (traversal-relative like `hinge near start`, so a mirror carries it
  without a handed flip). This is a language form, so it needs a parser and resolver
  (`placeAgainst` with the plan size swapped and the turn off the wall direction ± 90), `fmt`,
  Plan JSON and `describe()` `_authored`, a `place` transform, a conflict code for a
  `requiresWall` piece, a spec/SKILL line inside the `spec.llm.md` budget, and a byte-identity law.
  Worth building only if authors keep reaching for `against` for side-on pieces; the anchor
  spelling covers room edges, and `at` covers the rest.
- **`examples/tiny-house.arch`'s bed comment** still says the rule reads the north wall as the
  bed's back "(a plan-soundness card)". It is now stale. It was left alone because the brief keeps
  the examples untouched unless the rule leaves a real defect there, and editing it moves every
  `lint()` span below it. It is a one-line follow-up for the next PR that edits that example.
- A non-`fixedAspect`, non-services piece drawn transposed against a wall (a wardrobe
  `600x1800` with its end on the wall) is now judged by no rule. Before, this rule caught it, by
  accident and in the same words it used for the false positive. PS2.53 (a per-family depth
  ceiling) is the card that catches it.
