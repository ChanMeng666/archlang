# Plan-soundness: PS2.11e (kitchen and doors), PS2.46 and PS2.54 as built

## What shipped

| Code or form | What it reads | Figure and source |
|---|---|---|
| `W_HOB_UNDER_WINDOW` (PS2.46) | A `stove`, `hob` or `cooktop` whose footprint overlaps, with positive area, a window's span on its straight host within `fixtureWallTolMm` of the centreline: the band `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW` reads. A window with its sill at or above 2100 mm is not judged; a curved host files `curved-window-host`. | NKBA Kitchen Planning Guidelines with Access Standards, guideline 20 (cooking surface safety): "Do not locate the cooking surface under an operable window"; window treatments over it should not be flammable. Read in the NKBA guidelines PDF [V]. |
| `W_HOB_FRIDGE_GAP` | A fridge or other tall unit (`isTallUnit`) in the hob's own room inside either side strip of a cooking surface, `hobLandingMm` deep over the hob's depth (`clear sides`' strips, ADR 0029 §4), measured against the walls too, so a wall between them ends the measure (`hobSideGap`; the placement check reads the same). Equal gaps on both sides name the unit first in source order. | `hobLandingMm` 305: NKBA guideline 17 asks 12 in (305 mm) of landing on one side and 15 in on the other, read in the NKBA guidelines PDF and its consumer checklist [V]; a tall unit inside 12 in fails it on either side. The first pass took 300 (half a 600 mm base unit); red-team round 1 set the cited figure, since a lint threshold is a length and no grid module enters it. |
| `W_LEAF_TOO_WIDE` | A swinging leaf wider than `maxHingedLeafMm`: a hinged door's `width`, or each of a `double` door's two leaves. | 1219 mm: International Building Code (2018 and earlier) 1010.1.1, "The maximum width of a swinging door leaf shall be 48 inches (1219 mm) nominal"; its commentary: larger doors are hard to handle and not typically fire tested. Quoted by iDigHardware (2018) [S]; the 2021 edition dropped the sentence. The card said 1200; the source says 1219. |
| `W_DOORWAY_BLOCKED` on a cased `opening` | The door rule's landing, now read for every leaf-less opening after every door, inside the same rule (one rule per code, `test/lint-layers.test.ts`). An opening up to 1200 mm wide is judged as a door: any piece in its landing blocks it. A wider one (an open-plan join, the full shared edge an `open between` derives) is blocked only when the pieces in its landing leave no 1200 mm stretch of its span clear (`openingBlock`, `src/geometry/rect.ts`); its message states the approach depth, the stretch left and the shortfall. A counter, worktop or bar counter is not a blocker: across an opening it is a servery hatch (`examples/corner-cafe.arch`). Message names the opening by the rooms it joins; a derived `open between` opening's hint does not offer to move it. `repair` clears what the rule reads; the placement check behind quoted positions keeps out of every opening's approach (conservative: a quoted position never narrows an open-plan join). | As the door rule; the 1200 mm threshold is the coordinator's ruling on PR #283 (red-team round 1), a broad doorway's width. |
| The face datum (PS2.54) | `doorLandingRect` reaches `doorwayLandingMm` past each FACE of the host wall (half its thickness further than before), and `approachGapMm` measures from the face. One function, so `W_DOORWAY_BLOCKED`, the placement check behind quoted positions, `finish`'s furnish stage and `repair` all moved together, and `W_LEAF_ACROSS_DOORWAY` (which always read the face) now shares the datum. | — |
| `door double` | Two hinged leaves, each half the width, one per jamb, closing onto each other at the centre, both to the `swing` side (`doorLeaves`, `src/geometry.ts`; the pair `isDoubleDoorPair` already treated as one assembly). Takes `swing`; refuses `hinge`, `slide`, `open`; accepted on an `arc` wall (both leaves from the tangent at the doorway's centre). Every swing measure reads each leaf. A person passes through one leaf, so `W_DOOR_CLEARANCE` judges the leaf (`activeLeafWidth`: `double width 1000` raises what a 500 mm door raises) and a connector's clear width is one leaf's (`connectorClearWidth`, so `describe().access`'s `estimatedClearWidth` and `W_PATH_TOO_NARROW`, whose remedy widens the pair to twice the passable leaf). | ADA 2010 Standards 404.2.3: for a pair of doors, at least one active leaf gives the clear width. |

## Decisions

- **PS2.46, its own code.** Not `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW` with a second catalogue set: a hob is a
  low unit and blocks no light, the defect is fire and reach, and the remedy differs (put the sink
  under the window). One code per distinct check (cards §PS2.11e).
- **Every window is judged.** ArchLang does not model whether a window opens, which guideline 20
  turns on; a high window (sill ≥ 2100 mm) is excluded, as for the tall unit.
- **No dwelling reading (D6) for the hob rules.** The hazard is the hob's, whoever cooks on it, and
  RC4 (a café) is an acceptance row.
- **Any tall unit beside the hob, not only a fridge.** NKBA's landing area is blocked by any
  full-height unit; the wardrobe and bookcase are in `isTallUnit` for the same reason. A wall beside
  the hob (A7, RS3's side, RH2) would read the same guideline: filed as PS2.59.
- **The `double` kind was paid for.** Its spec text took 74 UTF-16 units, 28 of them paid back by cutting
  the door line's drawing-only aside "(nothing measured reads it)" after `open` is DRAWING-only (net +46);
  SKILL gained two sentences; the catalogue's `E_DOOR_KIND_CLAUSE` and `E_DOOR_KIND_CURVED` say
  where it stands. The law: 23 plans that write no `double` door, pinned against `8c2d6150`'s
  `src/` (whole payload, every storey, with diagnostics), are byte-identical; the other 17 (the
  edited examples and `off-path-bypass`) are pinned without `lint()` on `main`'s source, rebuilt
  from the branch's file and held to `main`'s bytes, against `2c7ca200`'s `src/` (SVG, `describe()`,
  diagnostics), so the `doorLeaves` refactor is covered there too; `format` and Plan JSON
  round-trip it. Without the kind, `W_LEAF_TOO_WIDE`'s only clean remedies were a sliding door or a
  narrower one: the C2 pier and eight wide leaves in the examples are the shape it replaces.
- **Wardrobe front ≥ 600: nothing more ships here.** `clear front 600` is the declaration (PS3.7,
  `W_FURN_CLEAR_FRONT`); a default is ruled out (cards §PS2.11e, PS3.7: `garden-loft` cannot hold
  one). A profile figure needs a source; none read so far gives a wardrobe front, though Approved
  Document M4(2)'s bed access zones would catch OA4, CA4 and RH4 from the bed's side: card PS2.60.
- **PS2.53 left open.** No sourced per-family depth ceiling for a wardrobe or bookcase was found.
- **Red-team round 1 (PR #283), three rulings.** (1) Cased openings: up to 1200 mm, a door's
  reading; wider, blocked only below a 1200 mm clear stretch, so a dining table 300 mm off an
  open-plan join is silent. The first pass's rule-dodging edits are reverted: `relational`'s table
  back to `inset 300` (and its walls-from-rooms twin), `hillside-villa`'s living/dining opening back
  to 3000 (its TV unit leaves 1750 mm clear, so it was no defect). (2) The hob figure is the cited
  305 mm, and only a tall unit in the hob's room, with no wall between, is read. The windows the
  first pass narrowed are back (`tiny-house`'s `n_galley` 900, `hillside-villa`'s east kitchen
  window 1000) and the hobs moved, as the hint says: `tiny-house`'s stove past the window's end
  with a 500 mm slimline fridge before the bed; `hillside-villa`'s stove to the window-free end of
  its run, the dishwasher under the window, the fridge onto the kitchen's north wall. At 305,
  `garden-house` and `terrace-row` (hob and fridge exactly 300 mm apart) are fixed too. (3) A
  `double` door is passed through one leaf. Re-checked under `accessibility-advisory`:
  `classroom-block`'s `d_front` 1400 (700 mm leaves, under 850) and `gallery-l`'s `entry` 1500
  (750 mm leaves; 690 mm clear, under even the default 700 mm path) widen to 2000, the 1000 mm
  leaves `classroom-block`'s header promises, with `gallery-l`'s jamb-to-jamb dim redrawn;
  `aquarium`'s `d_rot_s` 1800 (840 mm clear, under the profile's 900 mm path) widens to 2000.
  The other doubles pass the profile's door minimum; `library`'s profile path finding is the
  hinged WC-lobby door's, as before. Also: the hob hint quotes a window's id only when authored
  (`RWindow._idAuthored`); `W_FURNITURE_OVER_WINDOW`'s same habit is card PS2.61.

## The face datum and the eval judge (D25, ratified 2026-10-26)

`eval/run.ts` counts `W_DOORWAY_BLOCKED` as a physical failure. Three holdout goldens now raise it
(dims-auto-cottage and galley-kitchen by the face datum, compact-studio by the opening reading).
`lintWarnings` moved under D19 for seven rows (dims-auto-cottage 1 → 2, core-and-shell 1 → 2,
three-bed-2bath 7 → 8, galley-kitchen 1 → 2, compact-studio 4 → 5, sized-kitchen-flat 3 → 4,
sized-wet-room 3 → 4). The coordinator ruled the face datum stands: the three judgments
(`semanticPass` false, `physicalWarnings` 1, the failure line) are written with a per-row note in the
fixture's `notes`, `JUDGE_VERSION` unchanged, recorded as D25, ratified by the owner on 2026-10-26;
its reverse-if returns `W_DOORWAY_BLOCKED` alone to the centreline. The round-1 ruling on wide
openings keeps all three: their door and opening are 800 and 1000 mm.

CI's offline `eval:ci` step (`npm run eval:ci`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml`) writes
`eval/results.md` and then exits 1 when `summary.semanticPass !== summary.total` (`eval/run.ts`):
an all-goldens gate, not a pass rate. With the three judgments it prints `✗ 3 golden(s) regressed`
and exits 1 at 23/26. The gate is the owner's and is left as it is. The committed scorecard is
regenerated by that command; besides the three rows and D19's four `lintWarnings` bumps, it picks
up drift `main` already carried since its last regeneration (`7134d034`): the same step on `main`'s
tree gives the same counts on every other row.
