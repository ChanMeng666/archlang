## 2. The corner

The walking line is drawn by `acrossLanding` (`src/elements/vertical-glyphs.ts`). At a corner it runs straight ahead
off the flight to where it meets the next flight's centreline, bends at a right angle, and runs on. In a half-turn it
goes out to the landing's middle, across, and back.

**"Along the walking line" cannot mean the length of that line.** A half-turn landing 2200 × 500 has a walking line of
250 + 1200 + 250 = 1700 mm, which passes a landing a person has 500 mm of. A length measured to the bend is half the
landing.

So a landing is read **leg by leg**: along the flight that reaches it, from its top step, and along the flight that
leaves it, back from its bottom step, each as far as the landing runs. That is the reading the foot and head already
have (a flight is stepped off straight ahead), and the one the source gives: "landings the width and length at least
as great as the smallest width of the flight" (Approved Document K (2013) 1.20).

**A corner cannot be shorter than `width`.** `E_STAIR_PIECES` already asks each flight to meet its landing along its
whole width, so the edge the first flight meets is at least `width` long, and so is the edge the second meets. At a
corner those are the landing's two sides. Run on `main`: a 1000 × 700 corner under flights 1000 wide is refused, *flight
2, 1000 mm wide and centred in its footprint, does not meet piece 2 along its whole width*. So:

| Landing | Legs | What can report |
|---|---|---|
| In line, or a jog | One axis | Its length under `width`; a wall |
| Half-turn | One axis, both flights on one edge | Its depth under `width`; a wall. The leg across is at least two flights wide, because flights may not overlap |
| Corner | Two axes | A wall across either leg; outside a dwelling, a leg under 1200 mm. Never a length under `width`: the resolver got there before lint |

## 4. `shape` shortcuts: not built

The value is real but small: about 70 characters saved on a statement written twice, by agents, who paste the block.

- **It reaches two of a stair's eight orientations.** A stair has four entry sides and two hands. `shape quarter-turn
  left|right` gives the hand; the entry side would come from the long-axis convention, which is exactly what the block
  form was built to escape. The issue's own `size 2500x2100` is landscape, so by that convention it would enter from
  the right, not as its flight 1 does. The rest would need `place`, or an `entry` clause, a separate open item.
- **The compiler would be choosing.** It needs a fill rule: a landing `width` square, flights taking the rest. Then
  every hint that says "each flight's `size`" (`W_STAIR_TOO_STEEP`, this card's landing hint) points at a word the
  source does not have, and must branch.
- **It costs more than this card has.** No resolver path is needed, since it can feed `resolveBlock`. Sizes are
  expressions, so the expansion is at resolve, not parse. It still needs grammar, `gen:*`, `format`, the printer,
  `src/cursor.ts`, hover, completion and a byte law, and at least 40 units of spec, against a card budget under 15.
- **No one asked twice.** #362 lists it as "Later". G10 §8 set the trigger at a second ask, and none has arrived.

Reverse if: a second ask names it, or a measured run shows authors failing `E_STAIR_PIECES` often enough that a
shorter form would pay. The `entry` clause should be decided with it.

## 5. Winders: not built

Here the value is larger: the issue says catalogue plans often have them, and a plan with winders still misstates its
going. The cost is also larger, and a part-built form is worse than none.

- A tapered step needs a going measured on the walking line, a figure at the narrow end, and a non-rectangular piece.
- Each profile's figures must be read from its source (AD K, IRC, GB 50096), as the others were, and a default chosen.
  None has been read. Writing them from memory would break "figures stay as the profiles state them".
- A `winders 3` that drew and left lint alone would state something lint then contradicts. One that only counted risers
  would pass stairs nothing had measured, which turns today's false warning into a false pass.

What stands today is the strict reading, worked on a real case. At 2750 mm a quarter-turn with three winders has 9
straight goings of 220, 1980 mm, against the 2420 mm lint owes, so it reports 440 mm short. That is wrong in the
direction that costs a warning, not in the direction that hides one. This card adds nothing against it: a winder corner
drawn as a landing `width` each way passes the new check, which a test pins.

Reverse if: the owner rules that the false warning matters more than the build (G10 §11 question 1), with a step
geometry decision and the sourced figures in hand. That is about 2 days, as G10 said, plus the reading.

**No German profile.** The iron law holds: it stays out until its rules are read from the source. DIN 18065 has not
been read here either.

## 7. The hint, and the byte law

`W_STAIR_TOO_STEEP` was reworded by H4 (`b1739866`, `eb8c7b9c`) and restored by S2 (`e3e45339`). What it says now is
true: `test/block-stair-lint.test.ts` turns a 2420 mm flight, 220 mm short, into a block that clears. It does not
promise a block always clears, only that a landing replaces one going. **No digest moves for it here.**

The third class of `test/byte-identity-baseline.ts` was spent then, and these are the rows, all a `hints` string alone:

| Commit | Rows |
|---|---|
| H4 `b1739866` | K1: `test/defect-corpus/stair-safety/stair-too-steep.arch`, and `5-townhouse.arch` of the five first-contact reruns |
| H4 `eb8c7b9c` | `test/import-module-lets.test.ts`: `test/recovery-corpus/two-storey.arch` |
| S2 `e3e45339` | The same six K1 rows; `test/import-module-lets.test.ts`: `test/recovery-corpus/two-storey.arch`; `test/block-stair-byte-identity.test.ts`: `stair-too-steep.arch` |

The law for this card, on every payload (SVG, `compile().diagnostics`, Scene, `describe()`, `lint()`, the accessibility
profile, the circulation overlay, steps):

- A plan with no block stair does not move, on any payload. `minimumRun` with `L = 0` is untouched, and this card does
  not edit it; `test/block-stair-lint.test.ts` pins its figures in the old operation order.
- A block stair's drawing does not move: SVG, Scene, overlay and steps are the same. This is lint.
- A block plan's `lint()` moves only where a landing is short or a rising piece is passed under, and `describe()` only
  under `--facts clearances`, where turn rows are added.
- `lint --json`'s `coverage`, and the human `lint coverage:` line, move on every block plan whose stops match, because
  `block-stair` stops filing there: 435 of the sweep's 483 block rows. That is a report beside the diagnostics, on block
  plans alone. `lint()`, which returns the diagnostics, does not carry it, and `validate --json` does not print it.
